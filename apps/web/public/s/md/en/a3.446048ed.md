# AI classifies and drafts; people decide

Where a language model belongs in a financial product, where it does not, and why the figures should never come from it.

There is a promise repeated in almost every product that adds artificial intelligence: it will do the work for you. It is said of summaries, of recommendations, of answers to business questions. The promise is attractive and, in text tasks, it is often kept. The problem appears when it is extended, without further thought, to money.

The principle we used to design Rovaq AI fits in three clauses, and we consider it a condition of trust and not a limitation: AI helps to organize and to draft; figures come from records that can be verified; and a person decides every action.

## The risk of AI that invents amounts

A language model produces plausible text. That is its strength and also its risk: a sentence can sound exact without being so. If you ask it to summarize a conversation, a minor error hardly matters. If you ask it for an amount, the same kind of error changes a decision.

Besides, the harm is not evenly spread. A tool that is right almost always, but now and then invents an amount, has a worse cost than one that fails often: with the second you know you must verify everything; with the first you stop verifying and the error gets through. Trust is lost at the first report where someone discovers that a figure had nowhere to come from, and earning it back is slow.

## Figures come from the verifiable ledger

The alternative is to separate two jobs that tend to get mixed. One is understanding text: grouping similar conversations, proposing a category, drafting a message. The other is calculating money: adding up refunds, comparing periods, measuring how much revenue is exposed to a problem. The first tolerates some ambiguity and is reviewed by reading. The second does not tolerate it and must be repeatable.

In Rovaq AI, the second job is done by rules and calculations over the organization ledger, which is insert-only and keeps the origin of each record. Every important figure carries its class, its source and its cut-off date, as we explain in [the article on provenance](https://rovaq.ai/en/blog/where-every-number-comes-from). When something is not known, the system says No data; it does not fill the gap with an assumption written with confidence.

The [Ask your data](https://rovaq.ai/en/product/ask-your-data) screen is the clearest example. Today it answers with rules and with read-only tools, without a language model, and it shows the steps it took to calculate each answer. If it does not have the data, it says so. Whoever uses it can follow the calculation and repeat it; they do not have to believe a written answer.

## What AI does today and what it does not

Saying that AI does not create figures does not mean it has no work to do. There are tasks where a model is useful precisely because its output can be read and corrected:

- **Grouping conversations into problems.** Grouping with AI models is enabled per organization, and each group keeps its quotes so that someone can check they really belong there.
- **Classifying.** Proposing a category for a repeated theme, for a person to review.
- **Drafting.** Preparing the draft of a message to a supplier or of an internal note, which a person reviews before sending.

And there are things it does not do. It does not produce amounts. It does not decide what action to take. It does not send anything on its own. It does not write to your CRM, which in the case of HubSpot connects read-only. A draft is a proposal of text, not an action taken. If a draft comes out poorly, it is corrected or thrown away before it reaches anyone.

### A person decides every action

The last step is always human. In the [To decide](https://rovaq.ai/en/product/to-decide) screen, each proposal arrives with its evidence and its reason, and the person in charge decides what to do with it. The drafts that AI prepares are text that someone reviews and sends; nothing is sent on its own. How a proposal is debated and how its outcome is recorded is another story, which we tell in [the article on decisions](https://rovaq.ai/en/blog/a-recommendation-must-be-debatable).

This split is not a brake. A team reads a draft in seconds, but redoing a wrongly calculated amount can take days. Dividing the work this way puts the speed of the model where a mistake is cheap, in text, and human review where a mistake is expensive, in money and in actions.

## A rule for evaluating any tool

If you are evaluating software with artificial intelligence for your business, three questions help more than any demonstration:

1. Where each amount comes from: a record I can open or a text the model wrote.
2. What happens when information is missing: whether it says so, or fills in the gap with something that sounds reasonable.
3. Who carries out the action: whether a person approves it or it happens on its own.

A tool that answers all three well can still be wrong, like any other, but its errors will be visible and correctable. That is what we aim for by separating clearly what the model does from what stays outside it.

## What we do not claim

- We do not claim that AI makes no mistakes. That is why its proposals are read and a person decides what to do with them.
- We do not claim that Rovaq AI is an autonomous agent: it prepares packages and drafts, and a person decides and files every action.
- We do not claim that grouping with models is on for every organization: it is enabled per organization.
- We do not claim that data is anonymized. Redacting or masking a text is not anonymizing it.
- We do not promise any amount or any financial result as a consequence of a recommendation.
