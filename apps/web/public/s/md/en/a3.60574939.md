# AI classifies and drafts; people decide

Where a language model belongs in a financial product, where it does not, and why the figures should never come from it.

There is a promise repeated in almost every product that adds artificial intelligence: it will do the work for you. It is said of summaries, of recommendations, of answers to business questions. The promise is attractive and, in text tasks, it is often kept. The problem appears when it is extended, without further thought, to money.

The principle we used to design Rovaq AI fits in three clauses, and we consider it a condition of trust and not a limitation: AI helps to organize and to draft; figures come from records that can be verified; and a person decides every action.

## The risk of AI that invents amounts

A language model produces plausible text. That is its strength and also its risk: a sentence can sound exact without being so. If you ask it to summarize a conversation, a minor error hardly matters. If you ask it for an amount, the same kind of error changes a decision.

Besides, the harm is not evenly spread. A tool that is right almost always, but now and then invents an amount, costs more than one that fails often: with the second you know you must verify everything; with the first you stop verifying and the error gets through. Trust is lost at the first report where someone discovers that a figure had nowhere to come from, and earning it back is slow.

## Figures come from the verifiable ledger

The alternative is to separate two jobs that tend to get mixed. One is understanding text: grouping similar conversations, proposing a category, drafting a message. The other is calculating money: adding up refunds, comparing periods, measuring how much revenue is exposed to a problem. The first tolerates some ambiguity and is reviewed by reading. The second does not tolerate it and must be repeatable.

In Rovaq AI, the second job is done by rules and calculations over the organization ledger, which is insert-only and keeps the origin of each record. Every important figure carries its class, its source and its cut-off date, as we explain in [the article on provenance](https://rovaq.ai/en/blog/where-every-number-comes-from). When information is missing, the system declares it; it does not fill the gap with an assumption written with confidence.

The [Ask your data](https://rovaq.ai/en/product/ask-your-data) screen is the clearest example. Today it answers with rules and with read-only tools, without a language model, and it shows the steps it took to calculate each answer. If it does not have the data, it says so. Whoever uses it can follow the calculation and repeat it; they do not have to believe a written answer. This describes the general agent of that screen; the Finance mode has its own engine and we do not claim it is rules only.

## What AI can do and what it does not

Saying that AI does not create figures does not mean it has no work to do. There are tasks where a model can be useful precisely because its output can be read and corrected. What follows is what the product provides for, not usage results:

- **Grouping conversations into problems.** This capability is enabled per organization, runs in batches and a person reviews it. Where it is enabled, the grouping proposes the groups and the product shows the evidence for each problem when links are confirmed.
- **Classifying.** When AI is involved, it proposes a category for a repeated theme, for a person to review.
- **Drafting.** When AI is involved, it proposes the text of a draft, which a person reviews before sending.

And there are things it does not do. It does not produce amounts. It does not decide what action to take. It does not carry out actions on its own. It does not write to your CRM, which in the case of HubSpot connects read-only. A draft is a proposal of text, not an action taken. If a draft comes out poorly, it is corrected or thrown away before it reaches anyone.

### A person decides every action

The last step is always human. In the [To decide](https://rovaq.ai/en/product/to-decide) screen, each proposal arrives with its evidence and its reason, and the person in charge decides what to do with it. When AI is involved, its drafts are text that someone reviews before it reaches anyone. How a proposal is debated and how its outcome is recorded is another story, which we tell in [the article on decisions](https://rovaq.ai/en/blog/a-recommendation-must-be-debatable).

This split does not slow the work down. A team reads a draft in seconds, but redoing a wrongly calculated amount can take days. Dividing the work this way puts the speed of the model where a mistake is cheap, in text, and human review where a mistake is expensive, in money and in actions.

## Where the work of the model ends

The split can be checked at every step. A figure opens down to its record, a proposed category is accepted or changed, and a draft is corrected or thrown away. At none of those steps does the model have the last word, and at none does the result stay out of sight of whoever reviews it.

That does not make the tool infallible, but it does make its errors visible, and a visible error gets corrected. It is what we aim for by separating clearly what the model does from what stays outside it, and it is a useful question for any software that uses artificial intelligence: where exactly does the work of the model end and the work of a person begin.

## What we do not claim

- We do not claim that AI makes no mistakes. That is why its proposals are read and a person decides what to do with them.
- We do not claim that Rovaq AI is an autonomous agent: it prepares packages and drafts, and a person decides and files every action.
- We do not claim that grouping with models is switched on or in use: it is enabled per organization, and here we describe the capability, not results.
- We do not claim that data is anonymized. Redacting or masking a text is not anonymizing it.
- We do not promise any amount or any financial result as a consequence of a recommendation.
