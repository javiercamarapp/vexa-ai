# Your marketplace exports are already data: load them with provenance

The files you download from your sales channels are not a pending chore. They are the history you already have, and it can be loaded with its origin in plain view.

A common belief runs through teams that sell on marketplaces: once a direct connection to my channel exists, I will start putting my numbers in order. Meanwhile, sales are downloaded into one sheet, refunds into another, orders into a third, and every month someone pastes them into a workbook that nobody else fully understands.

The wait has a cost that goes unnoticed: those files already hold the history. They are not a step before the data; they are the data. What is almost always missing is not the source, but a place to load them without losing where each line came from.

## What can be loaded today

Today Rovaq AI receives Excel and CSV files. That is the way the data from your sales channels comes in: the exports you already download from each platform, in the format each one produces. Marketplace exports are imported with help from our team, who go over with you how each column maps.

Having support during the import has a practical advantage. Each channel exports its columns in its own way, and the same idea can show up as order, purchase or transaction depending on where it comes from. Settling that correspondence once, with someone who already knows it, keeps a misread column from contaminating months of history.

There is also a reading of conversations from HubSpot, and it is read-only: Rovaq AI does not write to your CRM. The connection is set up with our team and syncing is scheduled, not immediate. In addition, coverage is limited to what the HubSpot API exposes and may be incomplete, so it is wise to read those figures with their cut-off date and that limit in mind.

## Preview and per-row provenance

Loading a file is easy; loading it well is another matter. For an export to be reliable, the import must meet two conditions.

### First you look, then you load

The import includes a preview and a validation before anything is ingested. Before a single line enters your organization ledger, you can see how the file was read and what would end up loaded, to check that what was read matches what you downloaded. It is the cheap moment to catch that a date came in another format or that a column changed place. Finding out later, with three months of data on top, is expensive.

### Each row remembers where it comes from

The second condition is keeping the origin. Each loaded row stores which file it came from and which import it came in with, so that any figure can be traced back to the line that produced it. It is the same logic we explained in [the article on provenance](https://rovaq.ai/en/blog/where-every-number-comes-from): a figure is worth as much as its origin, and an origin lost in pasting from one sheet to another cannot be rebuilt later.

This changes the conversation when someone questions a number. Instead of reconstructing from memory which sheet was used, you open the row, see the file and settle it.

## Direct connectors: what does not exist yet

It is worth being clear about what does not exist yet. Direct connectors for Amazon, Walmart and Shopify do not exist yet: today no connection brings that data in automatically. That is why the route is the export, and why we set no dates or commitments for when it will change.

There are good reasons not to wait. A connector, when it arrives, will bring new data; but your history exists now, and it is exactly what lets you look back: seeing by channel, by product and by month where returned money concentrates, as we describe in [the article on refunds](https://rovaq.ai/en/blog/refund-leak-you-can-locate). Loading the history you already have today does not get in the way of a future connector. What does get in the way is continuing to paste sheets by hand in the meantime.

## How to prepare your exports

Before asking for an import, it helps to review your files with these ideas in mind:

1. Download the original export from each channel and keep it unedited. The file as it left the platform is the most direct evidence of its origin.
2. Note the period each file covers and, if you know it, the date you downloaded it. That will be the cut-off date for what you load.
3. Separate what you download by type: orders and refunds. Mixing them in one sheet complicates reading. Conversations are not loaded by file: they come through the HubSpot connection.
4. Go through the preview calmly and confirm that columns and dates were read as you expected before approving the import.

Start small: one channel and one complete period are enough to learn whether the file reads the way you expect. If the preview matches, the next channel loads the same way; if not, the error fits in a single sheet and is fixed before it grows. That is how the history becomes dependable, import after import.

With a couple of recent exports you can already answer which channel weighs most in your sales and where each figure that says so comes from. The history you already had is the starting point, with its origin in plain view.
